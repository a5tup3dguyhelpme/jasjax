disadvantages:

The "hallucinations" in code: Since language models are designed to predict the most likely word or instruction—rather than necessarily the "truth"—an AI might confidently invent libraries, functions, or entire lines of code that do not actually exist. This phenomenon of fabricating erroneous data is formally known as hallucination.

More time fixing than creating: Although AI appears to save time, surveys of engineering teams reveal that nearly 67% of programmers report spending significantly more time resolving bugs and maintenance issues now than they did two years ago, due to hidden flaws generated by automated tools.

The "Code Churn" phenomenon: Excessive use of AI has caused a surge in the rate of duplicate code—or "code that is written and then deleted or rewritten within a few days." Instead of reusing clean, efficient functions, AI often suggests duplicating logic, which clutters and bloats projects.

Programming without reading ("by coding"): There is a new technical concept known as "by coding," which describes the dangerous tendency of some modern programmers to simply copy and paste AI-generated code without reading it or understanding its internal workings—blindly trusting it until the system inevitably crashes. This reinforces the fact that while AI is an excellent support tool, logical thinking and human oversight remain irreplaceable.
